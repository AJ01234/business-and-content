# 32 — Founder Playbook

## 1. Start with the problem

Write the problem without mentioning the product. If the problem does not sound expensive, frequent or emotionally/operationally important, rethink the wedge.

## 2. Build evidence before narrative

Track:
- customer conversations
- repeated pain statements
- willingness to pay
- time/cost saved
- retention
- referrals
- failed experiments

## 3. Build the smallest high-information product

The goal of the first version is not feature completeness. It is to answer the most important uncertainty.

## 4. Make your founder edge explicit

Answer:

> Why you, specifically, are unusually equipped to solve this problem?

Use evidence, not adjectives.

## 5. Explain why now

Identify the changed condition:
- model capability
- infrastructure cost
- regulation
- behavior
- distribution
- market structure

## 6. Design the moat early

Ask which compounding advantage can emerge:
- network effects
- proprietary data
- workflow embedding
- distribution
- brand/trust
- technical differentiation
- scale economics

## 7. Build for the investor you want

### YC
Show clarity, speed, founder evidence, problem and large potential.

### Sequoia
Show exceptional founder quality, unique insight and transformational market potential.

### a16z
Show technology/category leverage and where the platform can accelerate you.

### HF0
Show prior execution and why concentrated building time creates an advantage.

### EF
Show unusual individual capability and founder potential.

### NFX
Show the actual network effect and its compounding mechanics.

### GC
Show why you are the right person for a difficult problem and why the outcome can endure.

## 8. Use the investor conversation as a test

A good investor meeting should make the thesis sharper. Track which questions repeatedly expose weak assumptions.

## 9. Measure what matters

Avoid vanity metrics. Use metrics tied to the mechanism of value creation.

For marketplaces, a16z emphasizes liquidity, market depth, matching, retention and unit economics. For AI workflows, track successful task completion, human override rate, time saved and economic value created.

## 10. Keep the Atlas loop alive

```text
Hypothesis → Build → Measure → Update belief → Rebuild
```

The strongest founders are not those who never change their minds. They are those who change their minds quickly when reality changes.
