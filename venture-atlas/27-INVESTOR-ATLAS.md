# 27 — Individual Investor Atlases

## Sequoia Capital

### FACT
Sequoia says it prefers to partner very early, seeks exceptional founders with unique insight focused on markets poised for large growth, and views investment as a long-term partnership. It explicitly says it is more interested in what might be possible than a working product or existing customers.

### OBSERVATION
Its current seed portfolio contains both frontier AI/infrastructure companies and businesses attacking established workflows: examples include Physical Intelligence, Inferact, Day.ai, Corma, Blockit AI, Vanta and Retool.

### INFERENCE
Sequoia's durable lens is not “AI” or “SaaS.” It is **outlier founder + non-obvious insight + large transformation potential**.

### Falsifier
If a broad sample of recent seed investments shows selection is mostly explained by traction, rather than founder insight and market potential, this model weakens.

## Andreessen Horowitz

### FACT
a16z describes a platform model with operators in marketing, talent, legal and policy and invests from seed through growth across multiple technology categories.

### OBSERVATION
Its public research repeatedly emphasizes market structure, distribution, network effects, metrics and category formation. Its seed fund announcement says early investments are bets on “a team, an idea, and little else.”

### INFERENCE
The a16z model is **technology + category expertise + platform acceleration**. The firm's support capacity is itself part of the investment proposition.

### Falsifier
If portfolio companies receive little measurable benefit from platform services, the platform-as-edge hypothesis weakens.

## HF0

### FACT
HF0 calls itself “the residency for repeat founders.” Public descriptions emphasize a highly concentrated, in-person environment where founders live and work together.

### OBSERVATION
The model deliberately removes normal life overhead rather than adding a large curriculum.

### INFERENCE
HF0 is effectively underwriting **execution compression**: prior founder experience lowers the risk that scarce residency time is wasted on basic startup mechanics.

### Falsifier
If first-time founders perform similarly to repeat founders inside HF0 after controlling for product/traction, repeat-founder selection is less central than it appears.

## Entrepreneurs First

### FACT
EF is a talent investor that can invest in individuals before they have a cofounder or even a defined company.

### OBSERVATION
The program deliberately creates a market for cofounder matching and idea formation.

### INFERENCE
EF is optimizing for **latent founder capability**, not company maturity. Its key underwriting question becomes: “Is this person unusually likely to become an exceptional founder?”

### Falsifier
If prior company-level traction is the dominant predictor of EF outcomes, the talent-first thesis is overstated.

## NFX

### FACT
NFX says it typically invests pre-seed/seed, focuses primarily on founding team and market opportunity, and specializes in network effects.

### OBSERVATION
Its public playbooks treat network effects as a design problem: the founder should know which network effect exists, how it starts, and how it strengthens.

### INFERENCE
NFX's differentiated selection model is **team + market + compounding defensibility through networks**.

### Falsifier
If companies without meaningful network effects are equally represented among its strongest outcomes, network effects are less central than its public thesis suggests.

## General Catalyst

### FACT
GC describes early-stage company building as core to the firm and emphasizes global thematic expertise, operating resources and radical collaboration.

### OBSERVATION
GC's investor commentary explicitly asks: “Why them on this problem?” and looks for product differentiation, important pain, unique insight, complementary talent and authentic motivation.

### INFERENCE
GC's model is **mission + founder/problem fit + difficult problem + institutional support + endurance**.

### Falsifier
If founder/problem fit does not distinguish GC investments from comparable companies, the model needs revision.
