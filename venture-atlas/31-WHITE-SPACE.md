# 31 — White Space

## White-space principle

A market with little venture activity is not automatically a good market. Low activity can mean low demand, bad economics, regulation, capital intensity or simply that the market is hard.

The Atlas therefore defines white space as:

**high pain + large opportunity + improving technology + low-quality incumbents + survivable constraints**

## White-space candidates

### 1. Legacy operational software in fragmented industries

Construction, logistics, property operations, insurance, field services and manufacturing contain many workflows that remain fragmented.

**Signal:** AI can act as a workflow layer over existing systems rather than requiring full replacement.

### 2. Small-business back office

Accounting, collections, compliance, procurement and customer communication remain heavily manual in many markets.

**Signal:** AI agents can directly complete actions rather than merely generate text.

### 3. Industrial and physical-world knowledge

Factories, infrastructure and field operations generate valuable data that is rarely structured for modern AI systems.

**Signal:** proprietary operational data can become a defensibility layer.

### 4. Emerging-market vertical infrastructure

India and other emerging markets contain enormous fragmented service markets.

**Signal:** software + payments + workflow can produce stronger economics than pure SaaS.

### 5. Trust layers for AI

As AI agents make more decisions, companies need identity, provenance, auditability, evaluation and policy controls.

**Signal:** trust infrastructure can become mandatory rather than optional.

## White-space traps

1. **“Nobody is doing this.”** Often means nobody wants it.
2. **Huge TAM without a wedge.** TAM is not a go-to-market strategy.
3. **AI wrapper without proprietary leverage.** Model access is not a moat.
4. **Regulatory optimism.** A regulation-heavy market can remain slow for years.
5. **Founder-market mismatch.** A good market is still a bad company for the wrong founder.

## White Space Index™

Score 0–10 for:
- unmet pain
- market size
- technology change
- incumbent weakness
- distribution accessibility
- defensibility
- founder edge

A high score is a reason to investigate, not a reason to invest.
