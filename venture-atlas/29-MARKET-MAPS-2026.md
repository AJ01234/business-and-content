# 29 — Market Maps 2026

## How to read this map

The categories below are not mutually exclusive. AI is a technology layer that can sit inside enterprise software, healthcare, fintech, robotics and consumer products.

## 1. AI-native enterprise

**Observed pattern:** Recent YC companies frequently automate specific workflows rather than sell generic “AI.” Examples include commercial underwriting, lending operations, regulatory writing, company knowledge, sales and agent evaluation.

**Investor signal:** Strong when the AI replaces a costly workflow and can accumulate proprietary context/data.

**Crowding:** High.

**White-space question:** Which regulated, fragmented or coordination-heavy workflows remain too difficult for generic agents?

## 2. Physical AI / robotics

**Observed pattern:** 2026 YC companies include robotics, manufacturing, industrial vision, waste sorting and autonomous CNC. YC's current directory also shows a substantial hard-tech cohort.

**Investor signal:** Strong where models become useful because they interact with real-world data and operations.

**Risk:** Hardware cycles, deployment cost, safety and capital intensity.

## 3. AI infrastructure

Examples in the current YC directory include inference, evaluation, data and agent infrastructure.

**Investor signal:** Strong if the infrastructure becomes indispensable as AI workloads scale.

**Risk:** Foundation-model companies can absorb infrastructure layers; differentiation must be measurable.

## 4. Healthcare + AI

Current YC examples include AI employees for primary care and AI-native drug discovery.

**Investor signal:** Large pain + expensive workflows + defensible domain data.

**Risk:** Regulation, clinical validation and long sales cycles.

## 5. Fintech + AI

Current examples include AI underwriting, lending operations, insurance adjustment, trading systems and performance-based payroll.

**Investor signal:** Excellent when AI compresses a high-cost decision process and produces auditable outputs.

**Risk:** Compliance, model risk and incumbent distribution.

## 6. Marketplaces

Marketplaces remain attractive where liquidity and network effects compound. a16z emphasizes fill rate, market depth, retention and unit economics as core metrics.

**Investor signal:** Strong when supply is differentiated, matching is frequent and liquidity improves with scale.

**Risk:** Multi-homing and incumbent network effects.

## 7. Consumer AI

The category is large and rapidly changing. The strongest products need a reason to exist beyond “chat with an AI.”

**Investor signal:** Stronger when the product creates a new behavior, identity layer, social graph, creator economy or persistent personal context.

**Risk:** Low switching costs and platform dependence.
