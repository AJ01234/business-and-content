# 28 — Cross-Investor Founder Patterns

## The recurring founder signal

Across YC, Sequoia, a16z, HF0, EF, NFX and GC, the common signal is not pedigree. It is **evidence that the founder can see, build and persist through something difficult**.

### Pattern 1 — Builder evidence
A founder who has shipped software, research, products, communities or businesses reduces the investor's uncertainty about execution.

### Pattern 2 — Problem intimacy
The strongest founders often know the problem from inside: operator experience, repeated customer conversations, research depth, or personal exposure.

### Pattern 3 — Non-consensus insight
The founder needs a reason to believe the market is mispriced, misunderstood or newly possible.

### Pattern 4 — Learning velocity
Early companies change. Investors therefore value founders who can update beliefs quickly without losing the underlying mission.

### Pattern 5 — Ambition proportional to capability
“Big market” is weak when the founder cannot explain how the first narrow wedge expands. The better signal is a concrete small start with an enormous reachable future.

### Pattern 6 — Complementarity
A team should reduce blind spots. Two founders with identical skills may be less useful than two founders whose abilities compound.

## Founder Edge Score 2.0

| Dimension | Weight | Evidence to collect |
|---|---:|---|
| Build | 20% | Products, code, research, shipped work |
| Problem insight | 20% | Specific non-obvious observations |
| Execution | 15% | Speed, resourcefulness, outcomes |
| Learning velocity | 15% | Pivots, experiments, feedback loops |
| Team complementarity | 10% | Coverage of critical capabilities |
| Market judgment | 10% | Why now, why this wedge |
| Ambition | 10% | Credible path to venture scale |

This is a research rubric, not a validated predictor.

## The anti-pedigree rule

The founder-background study in the YC research shows that observable background variables explain less than 4% of funding variation. The correct Atlas conclusion is not “credentials don't matter.” It is: **credentials are too weak to serve as a complete founder model.**

## The founder archetype stack

The strongest teams often combine:

**Technologist + Domain Insider + Commercial Operator + Relentless Executor**

One person can cover several roles. What matters is capability coverage, not titles.
